# Capability map

### Now
C1 — A Creator can sign up and sign in via Google/YouTube OAuth.
C2 — A Creator can connect a YouTube Channel via OAuth so the system can sync it.
C3 — A Creator can invite an Analyst to their Channel, and the Analyst can sign up/sign in via OAuth to accept.
C4 — A Creator or Analyst can research a topic and have an agent write cited, timestamped research notes into the Channel's research store.
C5 — A Creator or Analyst can hold a viability discussion grounded in the research store, request more research on gaps found, and save a viability verdict for an idea.
C6 — A Creator or Analyst can see the Channel's actual current schedule, synced from YouTube, as planning context.
C7 — A Creator or Analyst can turn viable ideas into a proposed schedule draft.
C8 — A Creator can review and approve a schedule draft, making it the Channel's committed schedule.
C9 — A Creator or Analyst can see a published video's actual metrics (views, retention, CTR/impressions) synced from YouTube alongside the viability verdict that predicted it.
C10 — A Creator or Analyst can browse research notes, verdicts, schedule drafts, and past prediction-vs-outcome comparisons for their Channel.

### Next
C11 — A Creator can manage multiple Channels.
C12 — An Analyst can work across every Channel they're associated with.
C13 — A Channel can have multiple Creators sharing ownership and decision authority.
C14 — A Creator or Analyst can browse calibration trends (prediction accuracy over time) across many ideas, not just one at a time.
C17 — A Creator or Analyst can ground a viability discussion in the Channel's own content history and comparable channels, not just topic research.

### Later
C15 — A Creator can delegate close guidance of research steps to an Analyst, reducing the Creator's direct involvement in Loop 1.
C16 — A Creator or Analyst can see deeper Analytics metrics beyond views/retention/CTR (e.g. audience retention curves, traffic sources, subscriber conversion), deepening whichever loop proves weakest.
